Levie 的观点本质上是给 AI 产品设计定了一个认知锚：AI 是工具导向的产品，而非拟人化的存在。对 AI builder 而言，这意味着产品交互设计应围绕「完成任务的价值」而非「像不像人」来构建。技术层面，它弱化了对通用智能/意识类问题的过度投入，把重点拉回模型的下游任务表现、可靠性、可解释性与成本曲线。在产品场景上，把 AI 框定为 utility（如水电般按需取用）也更利于定价模式、SLA 承诺与合规界定的讨论，避免拟人化带来的伦理与责任模糊。
Levie's point anchors AI product design toward function over personification. For AI builders, this means shaping product interactions around task completion value rather than how "human-like" the AI feels. Technically, it deprioritizes over-investment in general-intelligence/consciousness debates and refocuses on downstream task performance, reliability, interpretability, and cost curves. Framing AI as a utility (consumed on demand like electricity/water) also clarifies pricing models, SLA commitments, and compliance boundaries, avoiding the ethical and liability ambiguity that personification introduces. It's a useful guardrail for both product strategy and engineering roadmaps.

从 AI builder 视角看，这条讨论点醒一个核心问题：对抗 AI 抓取的"灰帽"思路（如反 OCR 字体、水印扰动）在工程上几乎无效——现代多模态大模型与 OCR 引擎具有相当鲁棒性，轻微的字体变形不足以造成实质阻碍，但会显著牺牲真实用户体验（可读性、屏幕阅读器兼容、无障碍标准）。对有内容保护需求的产品团队而言，更务实的路径是合法的权限控制、速率限制或内容协议（如 robots.txt、AI 训练许可协议），而非在渲染层做无谓的对抗。这也提示算法侧：鲁棒识别能力本身就是有商业价值的产品卖点，能"穿透"这类对抗性干扰的模型会更有竞争力。
From an AI builder's perspective, this discussion exposes a core truth: "gray-hat" approaches to evading AI scraping (anti-OCR fonts, watermark perturbations) largely fail at the engineering level. Modern multimodal LLMs and OCR engines are robust enough that subtle font distortions create no real obstacle, yet they significantly degrade real UX (readability, screen-reader compatibility, accessibility standards). For product teams with content-protection needs, pragmatic paths are legal access control, rate limiting, or content protocols (robots.txt, AI training licenses) rather than futile rendering-layer adversarial games. It also signals to algorithm teams that robust recognition itself is a salable product differentiator—models that can cut through such interference will be more competitive.
